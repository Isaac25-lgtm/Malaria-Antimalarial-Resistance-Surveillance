# HMIS OPD 002 — Outpatient Register

The source-to-canonical dictionary for MARS's outpatient encounter model.

Every canonical field below cites the form, the page and the **printed label**
it comes from. Nothing here was inferred from another form, from DHIS2, or from
what an outpatient register usually contains. Where the form is ambiguous, the
ambiguity is recorded rather than resolved.

### Stated purpose, from the form

> **Objective:** Used to record detailed information about each outpatient visit
> **Copies:** One. This stays at the Health Unit and preferably in the Out Patients Department (OPD)
> **Responsibility:** In-charge OPD

One register row is **one outpatient visit**, not one patient. The same person
attending twice appears twice, which is what makes column 16 meaningful.

## Columns 1–22

Ambiguity and sensitivity are recorded per field. Sensitivity uses the MARS
tiers: **A** aggregate, **P** pseudonymous case, **D** direct identity.

### (1) Serial Number — page 2 §1

> *"Write the patient serial number. Start with `001` at the beginning of every month."*

| Canonical | `serial_number` |
| --- | --- |
| Type | `varchar(16)` |
| Normalisation | Trimmed; leading zeros **preserved** — `001` is the printed form, and stripping it would break reconciliation against the paper book |
| Validation | Non-empty; unique within (facility, register book, month) |
| Sensitivity | P |
| **Ambiguity** | The serial resets monthly, so it is unique only within a facility-month. It is **not** a patient identifier and is never used as one. |

### (2) NIN — page 2 §2

> *"The patient's National Identification Number, or refugee number or passport number in case of foreigners."*

| Canonical | **Not stored in `mars_core`.** |
| --- | --- |
| Type | — |
| Sensitivity | **D** |
| Disposition | A direct national identifier. It goes to the identity vault (`mars_identity`, Prompt 8) and never appears in the encounter model, in logs, or in any API payload. `mars_core` holds only a pseudonymous `patient_reference_id`. |
| **Ambiguity** | One column carries three different identifier systems — NIN, refugee number, passport — with no type marker. The type must be inferred from column 6 (Patient Category), and that inference is recorded as an assertion, not a fact. |

### (4) Age — page 2 §4

> *"Write the patient's age in complete years if the patient is over one year of age. Write the patient's age in months if the patient is under one year of age and write clearly `MTH` after the age. Write the patient's age in days if the patient is less than one month of age and write clearly `Days` after the age."*

| Canonical | `age_value` + `age_unit` |
| --- | --- |
| Type | `integer` + enum `years` \| `months` \| `days` |
| Normalisation | The **printed unit is preserved**. A 3-month-old is stored as `(3, months)`, not as `0.25 years` — converting at ingestion would discard the precision the form deliberately captures for infants. |
| Derived | `age_days_approx` (integer, nullable) for banding only, computed as years×365 / months×30 / days×1. Marked approximate because the form gives no birth date. |
| Validation | `age_value >= 0`; years ≤ 130; months ≤ 11; days ≤ 30 — the form's own unit rules make higher values contradictions |
| Sensitivity | P |
| **Ambiguity** | Exact age in years is a quasi-identifier at fine geography. Age **banding** is applied at every analytical boundary, not at storage. |

### (5) Sex — page 2 §5

> *"Write the Sex (Gender) of the patient. Indicate `M` for male and `F` for female."*

| Canonical | `sex` |
| --- | --- |
| Type | enum `male` \| `female` \| `unknown` |
| Normalisation | `M`→male, `F`→female, blank/illegible→unknown |
| Validation | Only the two printed codes are accepted; anything else becomes `unknown` **with a source-value note**, never a guess |
| Sensitivity | P |
| **Ambiguity** | The form prints only M and F. `unknown` is a MARS addition for unreadable or absent entries and is documented as such — it is not a third category on the form. |

### (6) Patient Category — page 2 §6

> *"Record the origin of the patient if the patient is a Ugandan, write `N` for National if patient is a Ugandan, `R` for Refugee, if the Patient is a Refugee and `F` for Foreigner if the patient is a foreigner."*

| Canonical | `patient_category` |
| --- | --- |
| Type | enum `national` \| `refugee` \| `foreigner` \| `unknown` |
| Normalisation | `N`/`R`/`F` exactly as printed |
| Sensitivity | P |
| Note | Also disambiguates which identifier system column 2 holds. |

### (7) Residence — page 2 §7

> *"Write the Village, Parish, Sub-county/Division and District of residence where the patient stays. It is important for geographical catchment and distribution of OPD population and diseases respectively."*

Sub-cells (page 4): *Village*, *Parish*, *Subcounty/Division*, *District*.

| Canonical | `residence_district_id`, `residence_subcounty_id`, `residence_parish_raw`, `residence_village_raw` |
| --- | --- |
| Type | `uuid` FK → `geography_unit` (district, subcounty); `varchar(160)` raw text (parish, village) |
| Normalisation | District and subcounty are resolved against the published boundary version through the alias table. Parish and village are **kept as raw text**: MARS has no parish or village boundaries, so there is nothing to resolve them against, and inventing an id would be fabrication |
| Validation | An unresolvable district or subcounty is recorded as unresolved with the raw text retained — never silently dropped, never guessed |
| Sensitivity | **P at district/subcounty. Village + parish together with age and sex is re-identifying**, and is therefore treated as the highest-sensitivity field in `mars_core` and excluded from every aggregate export |
| **Ambiguity** | Residence is the patient's stated home, which is *not* the facility's geography. Signals must not conflate them. |

### (8) Next of Kin Name and Contact — page 2 §8

> *"Write the names of the next of Kin Telephone contact and relationship to Client to be contacted in case of any follow up or emergency."*

| Canonical | **Not stored anywhere in MARS.** |
| --- | --- |
| Sensitivity | **D**, and of a *third party who is not the patient* |
| Disposition | Not stored — not in `mars_core`, and **not in the identity vault either**. Surveillance has no purpose for a third party's contact details, and the vault exists for linking patients to themselves, not for holding a contact book. Any e-register extract containing it is dropped at the ingestion boundary. |

### (13) Malaria Tests — page 3 §13 ★ core to MARS

> *"Indicate Yes if client has fever. `NO` if client has no fever. Record the kind of test done i.e. `B/S` for microscopy `RDT` for Rapid Diagnostic Test, `ND` if no test was done. Write `POS` for positive result, `NEG` for negative result and `ND` if no test done under result."*

Grid sub-columns (page 5): *Fever (Y/N)*, *Tests Done (B/S, Y/N/ND)*, *Results (POS/NEG/NA)*.

| Canonical | `fever_present` on the encounter, plus one `opd_encounter_test` row |
| --- | --- |
| `fever_present` | enum `yes` \| `no` \| `unknown` — from the printed Y/N |
| `method` (on `opd_encounter_test`) | enum `microscopy` \| `rdt` \| `not_done` — from `B/S` \| `RDT` \| `ND` |
| `result` (on `opd_encounter_test`) | enum `positive` \| `negative` \| `not_done` \| `not_applicable` — from `POS` \| `NEG` \| `ND` \| `NA` |
| Validation | `method = not_done` **requires** a result of `not_done` or `not_applicable`. A positive result with no test is a contradiction and is rejected to a quarantine row rather than stored. |
| Sensitivity | P |
| **Ambiguity** | The instruction text and the grid header disagree on the codes. The instructions say the *Results* column takes `ND`; the printed grid header says `POS/NEG/NA`. Both `ND` and `NA` are therefore accepted and mapped to distinct canonical values, and the disagreement is recorded here rather than resolved by preferring one. |
| **Ambiguity** | The *Tests Done* grid header reads `(B/S, Y/N/ND)` while the instructions describe `B/S`, `RDT`, `ND`. The `Y/N` in the header has no meaning under the instructions and is treated as a printing artefact; `RDT` is accepted, as the instructions require. |

### (16) Classification (Tick) — page 3 §16 ★ core to MARS

> *"NEW ATTENDANCE: Tick if the patient has a new case of illness… RE-ATTENDANCE: Tick if the patient is a re-attendance."*

| Canonical | `attendance_type` |
| --- | --- |
| Type | enum `new_attendance` \| `re_attendance` \| `unknown` |
| Validation | Exactly one of the two ticks. Both ticked, or neither, becomes `unknown` with a source note — the form gives no rule for resolving it. |
| Sensitivity | P |
| Why it matters | Re-attendance is one of the routine signals MARS watches. It is **not** evidence of treatment failure or of resistance, and no MARS surface may describe it as either. A patient may re-attend for a hundred reasons the register does not record. |

### (18) Diagnosis — page 3 §18 ★ core to MARS

> *"Write clearly all diagnosis made. If more space is required, use another line. Remember that all diagnoses of notifiable diseases should be clearly starred (\*) by the Serial Number."*
>
> *"All diagnoses must be made according to the standard case definitions and Uganda Clinical Guidelines (UCG) provided by the Ministry of Health. The written diagnosis should correspond to one of the diagnoses listed in the Monthly Health Unit report (HMIS 105)."*

| Canonical | `opd_encounter_diagnosis` — **repeatable** |
| --- | --- |
| `sequence` | `smallint`, the order written |
| `diagnosis_raw` | `varchar(300)`, verbatim |
| `diagnosis_normalised` | `varchar(300)`, case- and whitespace-normalised for matching |
| `is_notifiable_marked` | `boolean` — whether the row carried the printed `*` marker |
| `hmis_105_item_id` | nullable FK, **unresolved until Prompt 11** builds the HMIS 105 item list |
| Validation | At least one diagnosis per encounter is expected but **not enforced**: the register permits a visit with no diagnosis written, and rejecting it would discard a real row |
| Sensitivity | P |
| **Ambiguity** | The form says the diagnosis "should correspond" to an HMIS 105 item, but the register is free text and often will not. Matching is deferred to Prompt 11 and every unmatched diagnosis stays unresolved and visible — never forced to the nearest item. |
| **Ambiguity** | The `*` is printed as belonging "by the Serial Number", i.e. against the row, while the instruction is about a diagnosis. A row with several diagnoses and one star does not say which diagnosis is notifiable. Recorded per row, not per diagnosis, because that is what the form records. |

### (19) Prescription — page 3 §19 ★ core to MARS

> *"At a minimum, the names of the drugs/devices and quantities given in accordance with the age and/or weight of the patient. Quantities prescribed should be written in the format: Number of units per dose x number of doses per day x number of days the drug is to be taken."*
>
> *"NOTE: In case of disability record the device given e.g. spectacles, wheel chair, walking stick, etc"*

| Canonical | `opd_encounter_prescription` — **repeatable** |
| --- | --- |
| `sequence` | `smallint` |
| `prescription_raw` | `varchar(300)`, verbatim |
| `drug_name_raw` | `varchar(200)`, the name portion as written |
| `units_per_dose`, `doses_per_day`, `days` | `numeric(8,2)` nullable — parsed only when the printed `n x n x n` pattern is present |
| `total_units` | `numeric(10,2)` nullable, the product, **null when any factor is missing** |
| Validation | A prescription that does not match the printed format keeps `prescription_raw` and leaves every parsed field null. Partial parses are never invented. |
| Sensitivity | P |
| Why it matters | Antimalarial prescribing is a Lane A signal input. A prescription is **evidence of what was dispensed, never of whether it worked.** |
| **Ambiguity** | The same column carries drugs and assistive devices ("spectacles, wheel chair"). A device is not a drug and must not enter medicine analytics; it is retained as raw text with the parsed fields null. |
| **Ambiguity** | No drug vocabulary is printed on the form. Mapping to a medicine list is deferred and every unmapped prescription stays unresolved. |

## Visit date

> *"Write the date on the first blank row. Nothing else is written on that row. Write in the format `dd/mmm/yyyy`. The month should be abbreviated in the form Jan, Feb, … Dec"* (page 2, Description of Columns)

| Canonical | `encounter_date` |
| --- | --- |
| Type | `date` |
| Normalisation | Parsed from `dd/mmm/yyyy` |
| Validation | Not in the future; not before the register's `date opened` |
| **Ambiguity** | **The date is a section header, not a per-row field.** It is written once on a blank row and applies to every row beneath it until the next date row. An extract that loses row ordering loses the date entirely, so ingestion must carry the date forward explicitly and record how it was assigned. This is the single most fragile part of digitising this register. |

## Outcome and disposition

**The register has no outcome column.** There is no discharge status, no
disposition field, and no follow-up result.

The only disposition the form records is column 22 — whether a referral note was
written. MARS therefore models disposition as exactly that, and **no MARS
surface may present an outpatient outcome**, because the source does not
contain one. Inferring an outcome from a later visit would be a derived
assertion, not a recorded fact.

## Deliberately out of scope

Columns 9, 10, 11, 12, 14, 15 and 20 are real fields on the form and are
**deliberately not modelled**. They are recorded here so the omission is a
decision a reader can see and challenge, not a gap.

| Column | Field | Why not modelled |
| ---: | --- | --- |
| 9 | Nutrition assessment | No malaria surveillance purpose. Nine sub-fields of clinical measurement whose collection MARS cannot justify. |
| 10 | Blood pressure & blood sugar | Same. |
| 11 | Need for palliative care | Same, and a sensitive clinical judgement. |
| 12 | Tobacco, alcohol | Behavioural data with no surveillance purpose here. |
| 14 | TB / HIV | **HIV status is among the most sensitive data a health system holds.** Storing it in a malaria surveillance platform, with no stated purpose and no consent basis for that purpose, would be a serious over-collection. Excluded at the ingestion boundary, not merely unused. |
| 15 | Leprosy | Same reasoning. |
| 20 | Disability | Sensitive personal data with no malaria surveillance purpose. |

This is data minimisation, not oversight. Each could be added later **under its
own governance decision and stated purpose** — the dictionary above records the
printed codes for every one of them, so adding one would be a modelling task,
not a fresh source investigation.

MARS also never stores column 3 (patient name and phone) or column 8 (next of
kin) in `mars_core`; see ADR 0006 and Prompt 8.

## What this model does not claim

The register records what was **observed and done**: a test, a result, a
diagnosis written, a medicine prescribed, whether the patient had attended
before. It records nothing about whether treatment succeeded.

No field in this dictionary supports a statement about antimalarial resistance.
Re-attendance, repeat prescribing and positive tests after treatment are Lane A
signals — they describe patterns worth investigating, and describing them as
treatment failure or resistance would be a claim this source cannot support
(ADR 0005).
